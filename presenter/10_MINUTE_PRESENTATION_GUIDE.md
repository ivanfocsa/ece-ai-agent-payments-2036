# Ten-minute presentation — four students

**Campaign:** Your AI Can Spend Your Money. But Who Is in Control?
**Country:** France · **Horizon:** 2036–2040 · **Client:** French Ministry of the Economy and Finance (fictional academic client)
**Audience:** Young adults aged 18–30.

Do not read from the slides or use phones/notes. Rehearse these ideas in your own natural English.

| Time | Presenter | Task |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–1:15 | Member 4 | Show opening video; do not explain it first |
| 1:15–3:05 | Member 1 | Explain 2025 x402 as a precursor and 2036 benefits |
| 3:05–5:05 | Member 2 | Stakeholders and first audience vote |
| 5:05–7:05 | Member 3 | Run simulated €50 wallet; class decides on €35 unknown seller |
| 7:05–9:05 | Members 2 and 3 | Ethical battle, second vote and frameworks |

## Member 1 — Technology
Key ideas: x402 is a current HTTP 402-based payment precursor, not a 2036 invention. Future agents may buy small API calls and data without long checkout processes. The big problem is control over the delegated wallet.

Suggested practice phrasing: "Imagine that your AI needs one travel-data request for ten cents, rather than a whole subscription. This can save time. But how do we stop an assistant from spending too much?"

Transition: "The technology affects many different people and organisations."

## Member 2 — French stakeholders
Users want control, refunds and privacy. AI and wallet providers want reliability and clear rules. Merchants need trustworthy payments. French supervisory bodies such as the AMF, ACPR, CNIL and Tracfin have different existing responsibilities.

First vote: "Would you allow an AI to spend fifty euros: YES, NO or DEPENDS? Why?"

Transition: "Now let us test your answer with simulated money."

## Member 3 — €50 live demonstration
1. Open [the interactive demo](../live-demo/index.html) fullscreen.
2. Set the automatic limit to €1.
3. Run a verified €0.10 purchase. Balance becomes €49.90.
4. Present an unknown seller requesting €35.
5. Ask the audience: pay, ask a human, or reject?
6. Click the selected branch, show the result, and reset if time permits.

AUTO-PAY: €35 goes to an unverified seller despite guardrails; remaining balance €14.90 after the small purchase. ASK: hold the payment for human review. REJECT: keep the funds, potentially delay the task.

## Members 2 and 3 — Ethical debate
- Protection first: consent, privacy, customer rights and redress are essential.
- Innovation first: too many approvals may make small transactions slow and impractical.

Second vote: "Should stronger controls be required for autonomous AI payments?"
Use all three lenses:
- **Utilitarianism:** Do the combined benefits outweigh likely harm?
- **Deontology:** Were permissions and agreed duties respected?
- **Rights-based:** Could the person understand, challenge, stop and recover from the payment?

Avoid claiming all French citizens think alike.

## Members 1 and 4 — Policy request
1. Explicit scopes and spending limits in every delegated wallet.
2. Checks and human approvals for suspicious merchants and unusual spending.
3. Receipts, evidence, complaints processes and clear accountability.

Last audience question: "Can an AI be autonomous while its owner stays in control?"

## Rehearsal

- Let the class participate in both votes.
